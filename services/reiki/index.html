What a distance session actually involves

You get comfortable somewhere you can lie down.

We connect briefly at the start to settle, then you rest for the bulk of the hour while the session runs. There is nothing to do, nothing to concentrate on, and no correct way to experience it. We speak again at the end for a few minutes so you can re-enter your day at a reasonable speed.

The hour, roughly

What the hour usually feels like.

01

First ten minutes — settling.

We say hello, you get horizontal, and you name one thing you would like the hour to be for. Or you skip that part.

02

The long middle — the session.

Roughly forty minutes. You rest. Eyes closed, camera off, mouth shut, the whole way through if you like.

03

Last ten minutes — landing.

A slow return. Water, a stretch, a couple of minutes of quiet before you stand up.

What to expect

A simple hour with clear boundaries.

What this is

A quiet distance session meant to support rest, breath, and receptive attention.

What happens in the hour

  • You arrive, get comfortable, and do less.

  • You close with a gentle landing so you can re-enter your day slowly.

Setting yourself up

A few things make it easier.

  • Somewhere you can lie flat — a bed, a sofa, a mat. Sitting is fine if lying down isn't.
  • A blanket. People often get cooler once they stop moving.
  • Headphones, and every notification off, including the watch.
  • Water within reach.
  • An hour with nothing scheduled immediately after, if you can arrange it.

What people commonly notice

Experiences vary widely, and none of the following is a goal or a sign that the session worked.

Some people report a lot; some report nothing at all. Both are ordinary.

  • A shift in temperature, or heaviness in the limbs.
  • Falling asleep partway through, which is common and completely fine.
  • Feeling more tired that evening, then sleeping unusually well.
  • Nothing in particular during the hour, and a quieter head the following day.
  • No noticeable difference at all.

What it is not

The boundaries stay plain.

  • Not a medical assessment or explanation.
  • Not emergency or crisis care.
  • Not a replacement for clinical care.
  • Not a health screening tool. Nothing about your health will be assessed or interpreted.
  • Not a reason to delay, stop, or change medical or mental-health care.
  • Keep your clinicians and keep your prescriptions.

FAQ

Straight answers, no filler.

Does distance work the same as in-person?

Distance is how these sessions are offered here. Practitioners differ on this and there is no clinical evidence to point at — you are welcome to be sceptical and still try it.

What if I fall asleep?

Very common. Nothing is lost. You do not need to stay awake for any part of it.

Do I need to be on camera?

No. Most people are off camera for the whole middle stretch.

What if I feel nothing?

Then you had a quiet hour lying down with your phone off, which was worth the time regardless. Feeling nothing is not a failure.

How often do people book?

There is no schedule you are supposed to keep. Some book once, some monthly. Nothing here depends on frequency.

Should I avoid anything afterwards?

No restrictions. If you can, take the rest of the evening slowly and drink more water than usual.

Can I book if I am under medical care?

Yes, and keep everything you are already doing. This sits alongside clinical care, never in place of it.
